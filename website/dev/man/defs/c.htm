Calendar Description - Scheme c

The Chinese calendar is a lunisolar calendar with 12 lunar months in a regular year and 13 in a leap year. Leap months are added by repeating a month, which is determined by the absence of a mid-season solar term in that month. The start of the year occurs just before the Northward/Spring equinox and is tied to the Southern/Winter solstice. Months start on the day of the new moon and have 29 or 30 days.

The algorithm used to determine the leap month is based on the rules described in the book "Calendrical Calculations" 3rd edition by Nachum Dershowitz and Edward M. Reingold.

See also the Wikipedia entry.

Use Case

  • China. This version of the calendar has been in use since 1645.

Definition

Record - Default Order
yearmonthlmonthday cycle segyear stem branch element polarity clmonth

The Record consists of four required Fields named year, month, lmonth, and day. There are twelve regular months in a year numbered 1 to 12. The lmonth field is set to 1 for these months. In a leap year, one of the months will be repeated with the lmonth field set to 2. Each month starts on day 1, the day of the new moon, and will be 29 or 30 days long.

The additional cycle field counts the number of 60 year sexagenary cycles since the year -2636 Gregorian. The additional segyear field is the year within the sexagenary cycle.

The additional stem and branch fields are the year stem and branch of the sexagenary cycle. The additional element and polarity fields are the element and polarity of the year stem.

The calculated field clmonth is a copy of the lmonth field when one of a leap month pair and an invalid or null value otherwise. It is used to display the leap month status in formats where it is required or omitted.
